# Space purpose

## What this space is for, and for whom

The product team's memory: what the product does, why it does it that way, and what we learn from users. Read by product, design, developers, support and AI assistants.

## What goes here

- One page per feature: problem solved, behavior, rules, limits, status.
- Specifications and their successive versions.
- Product decisions, with the date, the options set aside and the reason.
- User feedback and interviews, summarized by theme.
- The roadmap and the release notes.

## What does not go here

- Day-to-day development tickets: they stay in the issue tracker (a link is enough).
- Personal data of the users interviewed: anonymize, otherwise leave in the inbox with the reason.

## Folder organization

- `features/`: one page per feature.
- `specifications/`: one page per specification, linked to its feature.
- `decisions/`: `YYYY-MM-DD-subject.md`.
- `users/`: personas, interview summaries, feedback by theme.
- `roadmap/`: current quarter, next ones, release notes.

## Conventions

- Language: English; names of screens and buttons as they appear in the product.
- Tone: precise, without unnecessary jargon.
- File names: lowercase with hyphens.
- Feature: context, expected behavior, business rules, edge cases, status (idea, in progress, shipped), links to the specification and the decisions.

## What matters most

- Distinguish what is shipped from what is planned or considered.
- Keep track of decisions and their reasons, even when they change ("Previously: …").
- Link each piece of user feedback to the feature concerned.
- Verified pages: each feature page has an owner (its product manager), who verifies it at each release.

## Vocabulary

- MVP: minimum viable product, the first minimal version.
- Discovery: the exploration phase before the specification.
